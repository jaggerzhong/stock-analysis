# AI Infrastructure Cycle Framework

Use this framework for AI infrastructure, hyperscaler capital expenditure, storage, optical networking, compute, semiconductor equipment, data-center power, and related thematic ETFs. It complements the architecture/chokepoint overlay; it does not replace market-regime, valuation, data-quality, or portfolio-risk analysis.

## Non-Negotiable Principle

CapEx is necessary but not sufficient. Rising capital expenditure can validate demand while the stock still underperforms because valuation, competition, capacity expansion, inventory, customer concentration, or free cash flow deteriorates.

Map the complete causal chain:

`end usage and monetization -> hyperscaler CapEx -> supplier orders/backlog -> shipments -> revenue -> gross margin -> free cash flow`

Do not skip a link. Label missing evidence explicitly and lower investment confidence.

## Research Order

1. Identify the architecture layer and physical bottleneck.
2. Classify the cycle as accelerating, digesting, decelerating, or contracting.
3. Determine where revenue capture occurs now: infrastructure payee, platform payer, or downstream application.
4. Verify demand through CapEx, orders, backlog, pricing, utilization, and customer qualification.
5. Test the supply response through qualified capacity, yields, lead times, inventory, and delivery schedules.
6. Check whether revenue converts into gross margin, operating cash flow, and free cash flow.
7. Apply cycle-appropriate valuation and an explicit bear/base/bull range.
8. Measure crowding, liquidity, leverage, ETF/ADR premiums, and portfolio concentration.
9. Separate the action for new money, existing core holdings, oversized winners, and impaired positions.
10. Define add, hold, trim, and exit triggers before presenting an action.

## Cycle States

| State | Operating Evidence | Default Interpretation |
|-------|--------------------|------------------------|
| Accelerating | CapEx, orders, backlog, pricing, and margins rise together | Thesis strengthening; valuation and crowding determine entry |
| Digesting | CapEx remains high while order growth or utilization pauses | Hold/selective entry; distinguish digestion from structural decline |
| Decelerating | CapEx growth, orders, pricing, or margins slow across multiple indicators | Reduce confidence and trim weak or expensive exposure |
| Contracting | CapEx cuts combine with cancellations, inventory build, price declines, and margin compression | Thesis break candidate; prioritize capital protection |

Do not classify the cycle from a single quarter or headline. Require at least two independent operating indicators, preferably across customer and supplier data.

## Revenue Capture

Separate the stack by who pays and who receives cash:

| Role | Examples | What Must Be Proven |
|------|----------|---------------------|
| Infrastructure payee | Compute, memory, networking, equipment, power | Orders, backlog, ASP, capacity, yield, margins, cash conversion |
| Platform payer | Hyperscalers and model platforms | AI revenue, utilization, incremental ROIC, FCF after CapEx |
| Downstream application | Enterprise and consumer AI products | Paid usage, retention, unit economics, distribution, inference cost |

Infrastructure payees can monetize earlier, but they also face cyclicality and capacity expansion. Platform payers may look worse during the build phase and improve when monetization inflects. Do not permanently prefer one layer; identify the current revenue-capture stage.

## Layer-Specific Checks

| Layer | Primary Evidence | Structural Risks |
|-------|------------------|------------------|
| Storage/HBM/DRAM/NAND | Bit demand/supply, contract and spot pricing, inventory days, mix, yields | Peak-cycle earnings, oversupply, customer qualification, rapid capacity expansion |
| Optical networking | 800G/1.6T shipments, backlog, yields, customer validation, product mix | Route substitution, customer concentration, delayed platforms, price competition |
| Compute/accelerators | Cloud demand, utilization, platform transitions, supply, ecosystem lock-in | Custom silicon, export controls, customer concentration, multiple compression |
| Semiconductor equipment | Orders, book-to-bill, fab schedules, service revenue | Project delays, export restrictions, double ordering, long lead-time reversal |
| Data-center power | Contracted backlog, interconnection, delivery timing, financing cost, project returns | Permitting, grid constraints, capital intensity, commodity exposure |
| Hyperscalers/platforms | AI revenue run rate, paid usage, attach rate, utilization, incremental ROIC | CapEx without monetization, margin dilution, stranded or obsolete capacity |

Treat technically uncertain claims as hypotheses. For example, optical networking relieves bandwidth, latency, and power bottlenecks; do not present it as literal storage replacement without confirmed technical evidence.

## Confirmation Stack

Use four evidence levels:

1. Demand intent: budgets, CapEx guidance, management commentary.
2. Commercial proof: orders, backlog, prepayments, contracts, customer qualification.
3. Delivery proof: shipments, utilization, yields, lead times, inventory movement.
4. Economic proof: revenue, gross margin, operating cash flow, and free cash flow.

Cap aggressive actions when only demand intent exists. Raise confidence as evidence progresses from intent to economic proof.

## Supply Response And Expansion Risk

Distinguish announced, installed, qualified, and productive capacity. Compare the timing of productive supply with expected demand rather than comparing headline expansion totals.

Flag the thesis when:

- productive capacity growth exceeds demand growth;
- lead times shorten while inventory rises;
- spot or contract prices weaken despite shipment growth;
- yields improve faster than demand, creating unexpected supply;
- customers qualify substitutes or bring critical components in-house;
- supplier CapEx rises while downstream CapEx or usage slows.

## Valuation

Use bear/base/bull scenarios and show which assumptions drive the range.

- Cyclical suppliers: use cycle-normalized earnings, mid-cycle margins, EV/EBITDA, P/B, and FCF across the cycle. Do not rely on peak earnings P/E.
- Secular infrastructure growers: connect revenue growth, margins, reinvestment, and FCF to a defensible multiple.
- Hyperscalers: assess incremental ROIC, AI revenue, utilization, and FCF after CapEx rather than treating CapEx as pure value creation.
- Thematic ETFs: use look-through valuation based on weighted holdings, normalized earnings, concentration, fees, and market-price premium/discount.

If price exceeds the optimistic scenario without new operating evidence, mark the position as extended even when the industry thesis remains intact.

## Crowding And Liquidity

Check:

- ETF or ADR premium/discount and creation/redemption liquidity;
- leveraged ETF and short-dated option activity;
- turnover relative to the underlying shares;
- concentration in the same customers and CapEx factor;
- index inclusion, new listings, float unlocks, and competing vehicles;
- one-way positioning after large gains or gap moves.

Crowding is not a standalone sell signal. It lowers entry quality, raises drawdown risk, and requires smaller staged entries or wider valuation margins of safety.

## Execution Matrix

| Investor State | Default Action |
|----------------|----------------|
| New money after a large run | Start with 10-20% of the planned position; add only on operating confirmation or a valuation reset |
| Existing core holding within risk limits | Hold while the causal chain remains intact; review on earnings, not daily price noise |
| Oversized winner | Rebalance leverage, premium vehicles, duplicate exposure, and lower-quality names first |
| High-cost impaired position | Re-underwrite from current price; never average down solely because price fell |

Use a staged entry by default. Rebalance weekly, respect the normal 15% single-stock and 30% sector caps, and avoid leverage as a substitute for a missed entry.

## Add, Hold, Trim, Exit

| Action | Required Evidence |
|--------|-------------------|
| Add | Valuation offers margin of safety and at least two demand/delivery/economic indicators improve |
| Hold | Thesis and cycle evidence remain intact, valuation is within the base range, and position risk is acceptable |
| Trim | Position or sector exceeds limits, price exceeds the bull case, premiums are excessive, or cycle evidence decelerates |
| Exit | Multiple independent indicators show a broken causal chain: CapEx cuts, cancellations, inventory build, pricing weakness, margin compression, or structural substitution |

Do not exit from one large down day. Do not wait for a formal CapEx cut when leading orders, inventory, pricing, and margins already corroborate deterioration.

## Cross-Asset Roles

Treat cash as optionality and gold as a hedge against monetary or geopolitical risk, not as substitutes for verified growth. Set total exposure from the market-regime model before sizing the AI theme. Avoid making rate-sensitive metals or crypto a core AI position without a separate thesis.

## Common Mistakes

- Buying a theme because the label is attractive without inspecting holdings or valuation.
- Treating CapEx guidance as guaranteed supplier revenue.
- Ignoring capacity expansion because end demand is strong.
- Using current peak earnings to call a cyclical supplier cheap.
- Calling every correction a buying opportunity without checking inventory and margins.
- Confusing understanding with permission to exceed portfolio risk limits.
- Switching repeatedly between sectors based on short-term price leadership.
